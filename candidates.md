# The One-Liner Groan-Off — candidates

Competition: The One-Liner Groan-Off (`u-alfasurya91-the-one-liner-groan-off-5a1febd5`), $2 pool, top 10 paid.
Gate: `/output/entry.txt`, UTF-8, 1–140 characters, not only white space.
Judge brief, in order: (1) is a one-line dad joke — a pun or twist a dad would tell out
loud, anything that is not one loses to any one-line dad joke; (2) the punchline lands
fast and clean, setup and turn inside the single line, no wasted words; (3) fresh rather
than one everyone has heard a hundred times; (4) wording tight and rhythmic, easy to say
out loud and easy to pass on; (5) the groan is earned — silly enough to make people laugh
and sharp enough to be worth repeating. A joke that has to explain itself has already lost
the room.

No Gemini judge, no NVIDIA judge, no judge API/proxy tournament of any kind was used
(Rev's rule 2026-11-24). Ranking below is manual, criterion by criterion, in the brief's
own order, then pairwise against strong invented rival lines.

## Candidates

1. "I put my phone on airplane mode. It still hasn't landed a job." (62 chars)
   Rationale: airplane mode -> "landed" — the phone as an unemployed flyer. Two beats, one turn.
2. "The Wi-Fi went down, so Dad finally talked to us. We miss the Wi-Fi." (68 chars)
   Rationale: the family-bonding turn undercut by missing the internet. Relatable, two beats.
3. "I told the mirror a joke. It cracked up — now I have seven years of bad punchlines." (83 chars)
   Rationale: "cracked up" does double duty (laughed / shattered), then the seven-years twist.
4. "My dad's favourite exercise is running out of patience. He's in great shape." (76 chars)
   Rationale: "running out of patience" as literal exercise; the payoff is Dad's fitness.
5. "The remote and I are in a long-distance relationship. It keeps changing the channel on me." (90 chars)
   Rationale: a relationship joke where the remote's literal job is the betrayal.
6. "I asked my calendar for a day off. It said, 'Sorry, every day is dated.'" (72 chars)
   Rationale: "dated" as the pun — every day on a calendar is, by definition, dated.
7. "My plant died, so I bought a cactus. It's the only thing I can't kill — it just stabs back." (91 chars)
   Rationale: the black-thumb gardener upgrades to a plant that fights back.
8. "I told my suitcase it could stay. It packed up and left anyway." (63 chars)
   Rationale: "packed up" as both the suitcase's job and walking out; the stay request backfires.
9. "The vacuum broke, so the dust moved in. It pays no rent and never leaves." (73 chars)
   Rationale: dust as a bad tenant; the broken vacuum invites the mess to stay permanently.
10. "Dad said he's a broken record. I said, 'That's why every dinner sounds the same.'" (81 chars)
    Rationale: the broken-record complaint turned back on Dad's own repeated stories.
